02 History for You

The Battle of Hastings, 1066

On 14 October, William of Normandy beat King Harold Godwinson in a single day’s fighting near a small Sussex town, ending nearly six centuries of Anglo-Saxon rule. Harold had just force-marched his army the length of England after crushing a Viking invasion up north — then turned around and met William’s fleet with no rest at all.

Still with us: nearly every English word for meat at the table — beef, pork, mutton — comes from Norman French, while the animals in the field kept their old Anglo-Saxon names — cow, pig, sheep. A class line drawn that afternoon, still sitting in the language a thousand years later.
