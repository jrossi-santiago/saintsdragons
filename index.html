How one day goes

A real day from the archive, exactly as it was sent.

  1. 01

    During your day · for you

    You read the history

    The Battle of Hastings, 1066. Harold Godwinson drove his household troops 185 miles in five days, destroyed a Norwegian army at Stamford Bridge, then marched the length of England again and stood in the shield wall himself. Two ways of making war met on a ridge and neither could undo the other, so it ran from nine in the morning until dusk.

    Written like a friend explaining it, not a textbook. Read it before you leave work and you'll have it by bedtime.

  2. 02

    Bedtime · read aloud

    They hear the same idea as a story

    The Lion and the Mouse. A lion laughs at a mouse who promises to help him one day. The day comes, the net holds him, and the small one everybody counted out chews him free.

    Each day tells you in one line why the two go together: “Harold marched the length of England twice in three weeks and still stood in the shield wall himself. We don't get to sit out the fight because we're tired — we show up anyway, and that is most of what courage actually is.”

Tomorrow, a new one. A new date, a new history, a new story — the same shape every day, so the habit does the remembering for you.
